# Journal Structure Variants

Use this module when adapting a first draft to a target journal. Always check the current official Guide for Authors before finalization.

## Full research article

Typical order:

- Title page
- Abstract
- Keywords
- Introduction
- Materials and Methods
- Results
- Discussion
- Conclusions
- Data availability
- Author contributions
- Funding and declarations
- References
- Figures/tables or separate uploads
- Supplementary material

Best for multi-factor agronomy studies with full experimental methods and several main figures.

## Short communication

Use only when the result story is narrow and the journal accepts the format. Compress Introduction and Discussion, reduce figure count, and move secondary variables to supplement. Do not hide design limitations to meet length.

## Methods-heavy or data-heavy article

If the manuscript's novelty is an analysis workflow, dataset or measurement approach, foreground reproducibility:

- Data structure and experimental units.
- Code availability.
- Validation and uncertainty.
- Reusable figures or tables.

## Nature-style high-impact structure

For high-impact manuscripts, the Results should carry the main narrative and the Methods may be condensed or placed later depending on journal format. Figures must be story-driven, not variable-by-variable.

Use this structure only when the evidence supports a broad claim. Do not force a routine field trial into a high-impact format by overstating mechanism or novelty.

## Elsevier/Springer/Wiley agricultural journals

Common expectations:

- Structured or unstructured abstract according to journal.
- Highlights and graphical abstract may be required.
- Declaration of competing interest and CRediT taxonomy often required.
- Figures commonly uploaded separately at final submission.
- Data availability wording should match journal policy.

## Variant-selection checklist

- Does the journal allow combined Results and Discussion?
- Are graphical abstracts or highlights required?
- Are methods before or after Results?
- Are figure captions included in manuscript or separate?
- Are supplementary files uploaded separately?
- Is line numbering required?
- Is word count compatible with the current draft?

Keep format adaptation separate from scientific revision. A format change should not alter results, statistical claims or data interpretation.
