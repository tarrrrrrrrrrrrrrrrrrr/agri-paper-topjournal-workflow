# Manuscript Draft Template

Use this template after the project inventory, data/design audit, analysis specification and first figure set are available. The goal of the first draft is not beautiful prose; it is a complete, traceable manuscript whose claims can be checked against result tables and figures.

After this evidence draft is complete, use [nature-polishing-flow.md](nature-polishing-flow.md) for the argument-level and sentence-level polishing pass. Do not use polished language to hide missing data, weak statistics or unsupported mechanism.

## Draft order

1. Materials and Methods
2. Results
3. Figure and table captions
4. Discussion
5. Introduction
6. Conclusion
7. Abstract and keywords
8. Declarations, data availability and references

This order keeps the manuscript anchored to what the experiment can actually support. The Abstract and Introduction should be written after the result story is stable.

## Title

Write a specific title that names the crop/system, the treatment or management factor, and the central response. Avoid claims such as "mechanism", "optimization" or "regulation" unless the design directly supports them.

## Abstract

Use five compact moves:

- Problem or agronomic trade-off.
- Experimental design and key methods.
- Primary yield or growth result.
- Primary quality, physiological or metabolic result.
- Bounded implication.

Every number in the Abstract must appear in a saved result table or figure source table.

## Introduction

Use a four-paragraph structure:

1. Agronomic or quality problem.
2. What is known about the treatment, cultivar or management factor.
3. The unresolved gap that this experiment can answer.
4. Objectives and hypotheses, stated in terms of measurable responses.

Do not turn the Introduction into a broad review. Each paragraph should move the reader toward why this experiment was necessary.

## Materials and Methods

Include enough detail for reproducibility:

- Site, season/year, crop material and experimental design.
- Treatment levels, plot/pot structure, blocks and replicates.
- Sampling timing, tissue or plot unit, and sample handling.
- Measurement methods, instruments and standards that are documented in source files.
- Statistical model, fixed and random effects, comparison family, multiplicity method and software.

If a method detail is absent from the source files, leave a query for the author instead of inventing it.

## Results

Organize by evidence logic, not worksheet order:

- Treatment and interaction effects on primary agronomic responses.
- Yield-quality trade-off or co-improvement pattern.
- Physiological/canopy/light-use evidence.
- Quality, aroma, nutrient or metabolite evidence.
- Multivariate synthesis or exploratory model, clearly labeled as exploratory.

Each subsection should start with the biological conclusion and then give the supporting statistics.

## Discussion

Use the Results as anchors:

- Interpret the primary finding.
- Compare with literature.
- Explain plausible mechanisms using cautious language.
- State limitations that affect inference.
- Finish with practical or research implications that stay within the design.

## Conclusion

Keep the conclusion short. State the most defensible finding, the applicable treatment/cultivar/condition, and the practical boundary. Do not introduce new literature or new numerical claims.

## First-draft QA

Before calling the first draft ready for polishing:

- Methods describe the same design that the data audit found.
- Results cite every main figure and table in order.
- Abstract, Results, captions and Conclusion use synchronized numbers.
- No internal file names, local paths, worksheet names or revision notes remain.
- Causal words are downgraded unless supported by manipulation and validation.
- The draft is ready for a Nature-style polishing pass only after numeric consistency and claim-evidence-boundary checks are complete.
